September 21–23 • Autumn Equinox

Mabon in the PaganRabbit Tradition

Night of the Root and Balance

Symbolism

The second harvest and the autumn equinox. Day and night are again in perfect balance, but now darkness is on the rise. The fields have been cleared, the food stored, and we prepare to descend into the darker half of the year. This is a time of thanksgiving and taking stock of what we have gathered.

The Rabbit's Wisdom

As autumn deepens, the rabbit knows it's time to return underground. The nights grow cold and long. But before the winter burrow life begins, there is the feast of apples and root vegetables – the earth's deep gifts. The rabbit nibbles carrots pulled fresh from the soil and knows that what grows beneath sustains as much as what grows above.

Step 1: Gather Your Items

Simple Household Inventory

Celebrate the harvest of roots and fruits:

Apples
Fresh apples – the star of Mabon. When you slice one crosswise, you'll see the five-pointed star inside, a symbol of magic and balance.
Root vegetables
Carrots, potatoes, beets, turnips, parsnips – anything that grew underground.
Autumn leaves
Gather colorful fallen leaves – red, orange, yellow, brown. The visible sign of the turning season.
Orange & brown candles
Colors of autumn, of harvest, of descent into earth.
Dried corn or acorns
Symbols of the harvest complete and stored away.

Step 2: Build Your Mabon Altar

Create an autumn harvest display

Use a cloth in warm autumn colors. Scatter your collected leaves across it.

Arrange the apples and roots

Place your whole apples in a bowl or arranged artfully. Surround them with your root vegetables (washed but uncooked for now).

Light your candles

Place your orange and brown candles among the harvest bounty. Add any dried corn, acorns, or pine cones you've found.

Step 3: The Ritual

Step by Step

Part 1: Acknowledge the Balance

The Turning Point

Stand before your altar. Today, day and night are equal once more – but this time, darkness will grow from here forward.

This is not something to fear. This is the natural rhythm. Rest is as important as growth. Darkness holds wisdom that light cannot teach.

Say:

"Day and night stand in balance once more.
The light has served me well, and now I welcome the dark.
I honor both, for I need both."

Part 2: Light the Descent Candles

Light your candles.

As the flames catch, say:

"I light these flames as the days grow shorter.
I do not fear the coming dark.
I trust the wheel. I trust the cycle."

Part 3: The Apple Star Revelation

Hidden Magic

Take one of your apples. Slice it horizontally (across its equator, not top to bottom).

Look at the cross-section. Inside, the seeds form a perfect five-pointed star – a pentacle, symbol of the elements in balance, symbol of magic hidden in everyday things.

Hold the apple halves and say:

"Magic hides in the ordinary.
Wisdom grows in the common fruit.
I see the star. I honor the mystery."

Eat the apple slowly, seeds and all if you wish (apple seeds in small amounts are safe), or set aside the star-center as an offering.

Part 4: The Root Feast

Eating the Earth's Deep Gifts

Cook your root vegetables simply – roasted, steamed, or in a hearty soup. Let their natural sweetness and earthiness shine.

As you eat, think about how these vegetables grew in darkness, underground, drawing strength from the soil. They teach you that what is hidden can be powerful and nourishing.

Before you eat, give thanks:

"Thank you to the roots that grew in secret.
Thank you to the soil that holds and feeds.
I eat these gifts with gratitude and reverence."

Part 5: The Gratitude Inventory

What are you thankful for?

Mabon is a time of thanksgiving. Write down (or speak aloud) everything you are grateful for from this year – big and small.

Include:

  • What you have learned
  • Who has supported you
  • What you have survived
  • Simple pleasures that sustained you

Fold the list and place it on your altar, or speak each item aloud and then burn the paper safely in your candle flame as an offering of thanks.

Part 6: Closing – Preparing to Descend

As the darkness grows, you will go deeper – deeper into rest, deeper into reflection, deeper into the inner work that winter invites.

"The harvest is complete.
I have gathered what I need.
Now I turn inward and downward.
I welcome the descent. I trust the dark."

Let your candles burn. Keep the autumn leaves on your altar as the season progresses, watching them dry and curl – a reminder of beautiful, necessary letting go.

Step 4: Walking into Autumn

Late September and Beyond

Bake an apple pie

Or apple crisp, or simply baked apples with cinnamon. Fill your home with the scent of autumn's sweetness.

Take a gratitude walk

Walk outside and notice everything you're grateful for – the beauty of turning leaves, the cool air, the quiet settling of the world.

Begin letting go

As the trees release their leaves, consider what you need to release. What no longer serves you? What can you compost into new growth? Let it fall away.
