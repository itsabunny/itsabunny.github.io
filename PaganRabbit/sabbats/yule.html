December 21–22 • Winter Solstice

Yule in the PaganRabbit Tradition

Warren Life, Rest & Underground Safety

Symbolism

The longest night of the year. Darkness reaches its peak, yet within it lies the promise of returning light. This is the moment when the sun is reborn and begins its journey back toward warmth and life.

The Rabbit's Wisdom

Now the rabbits retreat to the deepest chambers of their burrows. This is the time for conserving warmth, keeping close to the fluffle, and preserving the spark of life beneath the frozen earth. The warren becomes a sanctuary of shared body heat, soft nests, and quiet waiting.

Step 1: Gather Your Items

Simple Household Inventory

Create a nest of warmth with what you have at home:

White candles
One or more white candles to represent the returning light. Even a single tealight carries the sun's promise.
Evergreens
Spruce, pine, or fir branches (or rosemary if you have it). Symbols of life that endures through winter.
Soft textiles
Blankets, cushions, warm socks – anything that builds your cozy "nest."
Winter foods
Root vegetables, nuts, dried fruits, warm porridge or soup – nourishing foods that sustain through the cold.

Step 2: Build Your Winter Warren Altar

Create your sacred nest

Clear a space on a low table or on the floor. This is your symbolic burrow entrance.

Arrange the evergreens

Form a circle or wreath shape with your evergreen branches. This represents the eternal cycle and life that persists even in deepest winter.

Place the candle at the center

Set your white candle in the middle of the evergreen circle. Surround it with soft fabric, creating a nest-like appearance.

Step 3: The Ritual

Step by Step

Part 1: Descend into the Warren

Settling In

Wrap yourself in your softest blanket. Dim all lights except for one small lamp or candle (not yet the altar candle).

Sit or curl up in your most comfortable position. Feel yourself sinking down, down into the earth, into the safety of the burrow. Take several slow breaths and feel the weight of winter above you – not threatening, but protective.

Say quietly:

"I descend into the warren. I am safe. I am warm. I am held by the earth."

Part 2: Welcome the Returning Light

In the darkness, light your white altar candle.

Watch the flame appear. This tiny spark is the sun being reborn. It is small now, but it will grow.

Say aloud or whisper:

"Welcome back, little sun.
Though the night is long, you have returned.
In the deepest dark, the light is born again.
I hold this flame with hope."

Part 3: The Inventory of Survival

What sustains you?

Think about what keeps you alive and well through hard times – not just physical things, but also emotional and spiritual resources.

Write or mentally list:

  • 3 things that nourish your body
  • 3 things that warm your heart
  • 3 sources of inner light you carry

Part 4: The Winter Feast

Nourishment in the Dark

Eat something warm and comforting by candlelight. Porridge with honey and cinnamon, a bowl of soup, roasted root vegetables, or simply toast with butter and jam.

Eat slowly and with full attention. This is the fuel that will carry you through winter. Honor it.

Part 5: Closing – The Long Rest

When you are ready, speak a final blessing:

"The wheel has turned to its darkest point.
I rest in the warren's embrace.
The light returns, slowly but surely.
Until spring, I keep my flame burning."

Let the candle burn down safely, or extinguish it gently and relight it each evening until the new year.

Step 4: Carrying the Light Through Winter

December 22nd and Beyond

Relight your candle daily

Each evening from Yule to the New Year, light a candle and sit with it for a few moments. Watch the days slowly lengthen.

Rest without guilt

This is the season of deep rest. Sleep extra. Stay in your "burrow" (home). Read, nest, and let yourself be still. The rabbit knows: winter is not for striving, but for restoration.
