February 1–2

Imbolc in the PaganRabbit Tradition

The First Nose in Spring Air

Symbolism

The first stirrings of spring deep beneath the frozen ground. Light is visibly returning, snow begins to melt, and the very first shoots of green pierce through winter's blanket. This is the festival of early light and awakening potential.

The Rabbit's Wisdom

After the long sleep of Yule, the rabbit cautiously pokes its nose out of the burrow. The air is still cold, but there's something different about it – a whisper of change. This is the first tentative exploration, testing whether it's safe to hope for spring.

Step 1: Gather Your Items

Simple Household Inventory

Gather symbols of early awakening and purification:

White & yellow candles
Candles in the colors of snow and the strengthening sun. As many as you like – this is a festival of increasing light.
Seeds or sprouts
A small pot with soil and seeds, or sprouting wheat grass, lentils, or cress on damp cotton.
Fresh water
A bowl of clean, fresh water – representing purification and melting snow.
White cloth or ribbon
Something clean and white to symbolize the cleansing of winter and fresh beginnings.

Step 2: Set Up Your Imbolc Altar

Cleanse your space

Imbolc is a festival of purification. Sweep your altar area from the center outward, symbolically clearing away winter's stagnation. Open a window for just a moment if you can, even if it's cold – let fresh air flow through.

Arrange your candles

Place your candles in a cluster or a line, creating a field of light. Set the bowl of water nearby.

Place your seeds at the center

Your pot of soil and seeds, or your sprouting project, sits at the heart of the altar. This is potential waiting to burst forth.

Step 3: The Ritual

Step by Step

Part 1: The First Emergence

Testing the Air

Stand or sit before your altar. Close your eyes and imagine yourself deep in the warren, warm and drowsy. But there's a pull – a call from above.

Slowly, like a rabbit cautiously emerging, open your eyes. Look at the unlit candles.

Say:

"I lift my nose to the surface.
I feel the air changing.
Winter is not yet over, but spring is stirring.
I am ready to begin awakening."

Part 2: Light the Candles of Increasing Day

Light your candles one by one, slowly. Each flame represents another minute of daylight gained since Yule.

As you light them, say:

"Light returns, bit by bit.
The sun climbs higher each day.
I welcome the growing brightness."

Sit quietly and watch the flames for a few moments. Feel the light warming your face.

Part 3: The Cleansing

Washing Away Winter

Dip your fingers into the bowl of fresh water. Touch the water to your forehead, your heart, and your hands.

Say:

"I cleanse myself of winter's heaviness.
I wash away what no longer serves.
I am refreshed and ready for growth."

You may also sprinkle a few drops of water around your space, or use the white cloth to wipe down surfaces mindfully.

Part 4: Plant Your Intentions

What will you grow?

If you haven't yet planted your seeds, do so now with intention. As you press each seed into the soil, think of one thing you want to cultivate in your life over the coming spring.

If your seeds are already planted or sprouting, simply touch the soil and name your intentions aloud or in your heart.

Speak your commitment:

"I plant these seeds in trust.
What is buried will rise.
I will tend my growth with patience and care."

Part 5: Closing – The Return to the Burrow (For Now)

It's still cold outside. Spring has not fully arrived. The rabbit retreats back underground, but not to sleep – to watch and wait with alert, hopeful eyes.

"I have felt the first breath of spring.
I return to my warren to wait and prepare.
When the time is right, I will leap into the light."

Gently extinguish the candles, or let them burn down safely. Keep your sprouting seeds in a sunny windowsill and water them regularly – watch them grow as the light increases.

Step 4: Continuing the Awakening

February and Beyond

Tend your sprouts

Watch them grow daily. This is your visible proof that life is stirring, even if snow still covers the ground outside.

Spring cleaning – one small area

You don't need to clean the whole house. Choose one drawer, one shelf, one corner. Clear it out, wipe it down, and feel the freshness of having a clean, organized space to grow into.

Notice the returning light

Each day, the sun sets a little later. Pay attention. Mark the moment you first notice, "Oh, it's still light out!" That is Imbolc's gift continuing to unfold.
