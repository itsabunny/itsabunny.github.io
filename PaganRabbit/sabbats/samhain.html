October 31 – November 1

Samhain in the PaganRabbit Tradition

Warren's Depth & Ancestor Paths

Symbolism

The final harvest and the Celtic new year. The veil between worlds is thinnest now. This is the time when we honor the dead, remember those who came before, and acknowledge that death is not an ending but a transformation. The year dies so a new one can be born.

The Rabbit's Wisdom

At Samhain, the rabbit is fully underground. The warren goes deep, with tunnels that connect the present to the past – passages that grandparent rabbits dug, chambers where many generations nested. The living walk paths carved by the dead. Samhain honors this continuity, this underground network of memory and ancestry that holds us all.

Step 1: Gather Your Items

Simple Household Inventory

Prepare for communion with the ancestors:

Black & purple candles
Colors of the thinning veil, of mystery, of the deep earth.
Photos of the dead
Pictures of loved ones who have passed, or representations of ancestors you never met but whose legacy you carry.
Bread, fruit, or dinner
An offering for the ancestors. Gather bread, maple syrup, fruit, or a bit of your dinner to share with the spirits (Liminal bread offering).
Dried autumn leaves
Symbols of the life that has fallen, returned to the earth, and will nourish what comes next.
A bowl of water
For scrying or simply as a mirror to the other side.

Step 2: Build Your Samhain Ancestor Altar

Create a space for the dead

Use a dark cloth – black, deep purple, or dark blue. This is a solemn, sacred altar.

Arrange photos and mementos

Place pictures of those who have died in the center. Add any objects that belonged to them or remind you of them – a piece of jewelry, a letter, a favorite book.

Set out the offering

Place your bread, fruit, or plate of food in front of the photos. Light your candles on either side. Fill the bowl with water and set it where candlelight will reflect in it.

Step 3: The Ritual

Step by Step – Best done after sunset

Part 1: Opening the Way

Thinning the Veil

Sit before your altar in dim light or darkness. Light your candles slowly, one at a time.

As each flame appears, feel the veil between this world and the next growing thinner, more permeable.

Say:

"On this night, the veil is thin.
I light these flames to guide the way.
I call to those who came before.
I open my heart to the ancestors."

Part 2: Speak Their Names

Remembering the Dead

Look at each photo. Say each name aloud. If you don't know their names, say "Ancestor" or "Grandmother" or "those who came before me."

For each person, share a memory, a quality they had, or a way they shaped your life – even if you never met them.

Example:

"I remember [Name]. You taught me [lesson]. You loved [thing]. Because of you, I am [quality]. Thank you."

Part 3: The Offering

Feeding the Dead

Take your bread, fruit, or plate of food. Hold it up to the altar.

Say:

"I offer this food to the spirits of my ancestors.
As I have been fed by you – by your work, your sacrifices, your love – I now feed you in return.
Eat, rest, and be remembered."

Leave the offering on the altar overnight. In the morning, return it to the earth (bury it, compost it, or leave it outside for animals).

Part 4: Silent Communion

Listening

Sit in silence for at least 10 minutes. Gaze into the bowl of water if you wish, or simply close your eyes.

Listen. Not with your ears, but with your heart.

What do the ancestors want you to know? What guidance, comfort, or wisdom do they offer?

You might feel a presence. You might have a thought drop into your mind. You might feel an emotion – grief, peace, gratitude. Trust whatever comes.

Part 5: Acknowledge the Dead of the World

Expanding the Circle

Samhain is not only for your personal dead, but for all the dead.

Speak a prayer for:

  • Those who died forgotten or alone
  • Those whose names are lost
  • Those who died in violence, injustice, or war
  • The animals and plants that have gone extinct
"I remember all the dead. May you be at peace. May you be honored. May your stories not be forgotten."

Part 6: Closing – The Veil Closes Again

As the night deepens or morning comes, the veil will thicken once more. But the connection you've made remains.

"The veil closes, but I carry you with me.
I walk the paths you carved.
I live the life you made possible.
Until we meet again, I honor you."

Extinguish the candles. Thank the ancestors. Leave the altar as it is for the next few days if you can, then gently pack it away until next Samhain.

Step 4: Living with the Ancestors

November and Beyond

Keep a space for them

Maintain a small ancestor altar year-round – just a candle, a photo, and fresh water changed weekly. They are always with you.

Research your lineage

Learn the names and stories of your ancestors. Interview elders. Trace your family tree. Every name you recover is a soul remembered.

Live as a good ancestor

Ask yourself: What kind of ancestor do I want to be? What will I leave behind for those who come after? Act in ways that honor the future dead – who are the living of today.
